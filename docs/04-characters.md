# 04 — Characters: The People of Twin Cities

**Contract:** Every person in this doc obeys `01-vision.md` — pillar 4 ("people, not vending machines") and the tone bible (R-rated energy, fade-to-black discipline, hard lines absolute). Every character below was written to pass one test: *would a TV writer keep this person in the pilot?* If a character ever reads as a quest dispenser, rewrite them or cut them.

---

## 1. The Hero Roster (10 recruitable supers — pick 3)

The player builds a squad of three. The other seven don't vanish — they keep living in Uptown, keep their schedules, keep their opinions about not getting picked. All ten are adults; ages stated. Powers are mechanically distinct: no two heroes solve a fight the same way.

**Romance legend:** *Open* = romanceable by any player character. *Oriented* = romanceable, with a stated orientation. *Exclusive* = demands monogamy; the gossip system WILL get you caught. *Friendship-only* = not romanceable, and richer for it.

---

### 1. Marisol Vega — "REBOUND"
- **Age:** 31.
- **Power kit — Kinetic brawler (momentum theft):** She doesn't take hits, she *banks* them. **Core moves:** *Absorb Stance* (soak an incoming hit into a stored-momentum meter), *Payback Palm* (dump stored force into one open-hand shot), *Wallride* (converts her own movement into a ricochet dash off any surface). **Signature — "Interest":** stores three consecutive hits without attacking, then returns all of it at 150% in a single screen-shaking counter. High risk, highest single-hit number in the game.
- **Personality:** competitive to the point of self-sabotage; loyal like a dog with a grudge; laughs loudest at her own worst decisions.
- **Day job / hangout:** bike courier and part-time mechanic at **Spoke & Dagger** on Lagoon. Off hours: roller derby practice, or the last stool at Moe's bar arguing about whose fault the derby loss was (hers).
- **Recruitment:** beat her. Not in a mission — she challenges the player to a 1v1 in the Spoke & Dagger alley after you break up a fight she was *winning*. You don't have to win, but you have to land the last hit clean. She respects the receipt.
- **Romance/friendship hook:** *Open, Exclusive-jealous.* Marisol dates like she fights — all in, no guard. Her last three relationships ended when she caught feelings faster than the other person and torched it preemptively. Get close and you learn she keeps her late brother's courier bag on the wall, unwashed, and the derby isn't a hobby, it's the only place she lets herself lose.
- **Is-this-boring test:** she can *feel* the stored kinetic energy of everyone around her — she knows who's about to throw a punch, who's about to cry, and who's about to kiss her, three seconds early. She still gets all three wrong constantly.

### 2. Frankie Larsen — "MOONDOG"
- **Age:** 26. Nonbinary (they/them).
- **Power kit — Gravity control (local weight editing):** Frankie makes things heavy or light in a bubble. **Core moves:** *Featherfield* (allies in the zone jump triple-height, fall slow — repositioning god), *Anvil Zone* (enemies in the zone drop to their knees, attack speed halved), *Sling* (grab any prop, gravity-yeet it as a projectile arc). **Signature — "Perigee":** collapses both zones into one point — everything airborne slams down at once for a crowd-clear crater. Fights around Frankie happen in three dimensions; nobody else's do.
- **Personality:** serene until they're absolutely not; allergic to plans; treats danger like weather — noted, then ignored.
- **Day job / hangout:** aerialist and instructor at **Sideshow North**, a circus-arts warehouse off the Greenway. Found on the trapeze rig at closing time, upside down, holding a conversation like it's normal.
- **Recruitment:** their rig gets condemned by one of Councilmember Odegaard's inspectors (see Factions). Save the warehouse — through money, leverage on the inspector, or one very public demonstration of what "structurally unsound" looks like when a gravity-controller is annoyed.
- **Romance/friendship hook:** *Open, non-exclusive by default* — Frankie is upfront that they don't do ownership, and the game respects it: dating Frankie never triggers their jealousy, but dating Frankie WHILE dating an Exclusive partner absolutely triggers the partner's. Get close and you find the serenity is trained: Frankie grew up heavy — a childhood they describe only as "gravity ran in the family" — and the low-G thing started as escape.
- **Is-this-boring test:** Frankie hasn't let their own body experience full gravity in four years. The arc where they finally stand at normal weight — and shake — is the quietest gut-punch in the game.

### 3. August Vang — "REVERB"
- **Age:** 25.
- **Power kit — Sonic (sound sculpting):** August hears everything and can throw it back. **Core moves:** *Feedback Lash* (a cutting directional shriek — the poke tool), *Dead Air* (a silence dome: enemies inside can't call reinforcements, casters can't cast, and the fight goes eerily mute — a design showpiece), *Bassline* (a rhythmic ground-pulse that staggers enemies on the beat and buffs squad attack speed if THEY attack on the beat). **Signature — "Encore":** records the last 5 seconds of combat audio and replays it as concussive force — the bigger the fight was, the bigger the blast.
- **Personality:** watchful in a way people mistake for shy; devastating one-liners delivered at library volume; holds grudges in perfect fidelity because he literally cannot forget a sound.
- **Day job / hangout:** live sound engineer at **The Foundry**, the black-box music venue on Hennepin. Between sets he's at the soundboard; after close he's on the loading dock listening to the city like it's a mix he's not happy with.
- **Recruitment:** friendship, not spectacle. August has watched capes grandstand his whole life and is unimpressed. He joins after the player does three small things quietly — no witnesses, no rep gain — and he heard every one of them.
- **Romance/friendship hook:** *Open.* August is the slow build: months of dry jokes across the soundboard, then one night he plays you a track he made of city sounds and it's — obviously, embarrassingly — about you. His baggage: he's the eldest son and his parents think the power is a phase between him and a pharmacy degree. He's never told them he can hear their arguments from three blocks away.
- **Is-this-boring test:** he knows everyone's heartbeat like a signature. In one scene he identifies a disguised villain in a crowd of forty because "your pulse lies worse than your face." He knows when the player's heart rate spikes around him. He's too polite to mention it. For now.

### 4. Ingrid Halvorsen — "WHITEOUT"
- **Age:** 34.
- **Power kit — Cold (the most Minnesotan power in the game):** not "ice blasts" — *heat theft.* **Core moves:** *Draw* (siphons heat from an enemy: they slow, she gains armor frost), *Black Ice* (paints a ground patch — enemies slide, her squad skates: allies crossing it get a free dash), *Shelter* (a snow-squall dome that blocks projectiles both ways — the "okay everybody breathe" button). **Signature — "January":** drops the local temperature to lethal-cold for six seconds; everything hostile freezes mid-pose like a photograph of a blizzard. Fights end. So do conversations.
- **Personality:** economical with words, extravagant with actions; competitive only where nobody can see; the person everyone assumes is fine, and never is.
- **Day job / hangout:** manages the **Uptown ice arena**; drives the Zamboni herself at 11pm because it's the only smooth thing in her life. Former pro hockey — one season, one injury, one long silence about it.
- **Recruitment:** the arena's rink chiller dies mid-heatwave and she's been keeping the ice frozen *herself*, secretly, eighteen hours a day, and it's killing her. Notice. Fix the chiller or get the money for it. She joins because you saw her working — she'll never say that's why.
- **Romance/friendship hook:** *Oriented (women), Exclusive.* Ingrid loves like she plays defense — total commitment, zero announcement. Her ex-wife Petra teaches yoga four blocks away (see Independents) and the divorce paperwork is done but the eye contact isn't. Get close and you learn the injury that ended hockey wasn't an accident: she Drew heat mid-game, on camera, in the era before that was survivable.
- **Is-this-boring test:** she is physically warm to the touch — always — because the cold isn't hers, it's everyone else's, held. The first time she lets a partner feel her actually cold hands is the most intimate fade-to-black in the game.

### 5. Theo Park — "STITCH"
- **Age:** 24.
- **Power kit — Speedster (short-burst blinkwork):** not a marathon runner — a sewing machine. **Core moves:** *Needle* (a straight-line dash-through with a slash on exit), *Basting* (tags up to four enemies at walking pace, then hits all tags at once), *Errand* (instantly repositions one ALLY instead of himself — the only speedster whose speed is a gift). **Signature — "Seam Ripper":** ten dashes stitched through the whole enemy formation in one second; the screen holds a comic-panel freeze of every pass before the damage lands.
- **Personality:** chronically early and emotionally late; funny as a defense mechanism, then accidentally sincere; treats every kindness like a debt he has to repay at 200%.
- **Day job / hangout:** delivery rider for half the restaurants in Uptown — everyone knows Theo, Theo knows everyone's order. Refuels at **Big Ronda's Nite Owl** diner counter at 2am, vibrating slightly.
- **Recruitment:** cheap to recruit, hard to keep. He says yes immediately — too immediately — and the actual recruitment is the follow-up mission where you learn he's said yes to two factions already and is quadruple-booked out of pure inability to disappoint anyone. Make him quit something. That's the price.
- **Romance/friendship hook:** *Open.* Theo flirts at full speed and retreats faster; the first real date, he shows up an hour early and does two laps of the lake to burn the nerves off, and you know because a jogger gossips about it. Baggage: his parents run a dry cleaner in St. Paul and think he's in grad school. The lie is eleven months old and compounding.
- **Is-this-boring test:** his perception of a romantic moment lasts, subjectively, about an hour. He has experienced every kiss of his life in slow motion, alone, and has never once told anyone that.

### 6. Maggie St. Pierre — "BALLAST"
- **Age:** 38.
- **Power kit — Density tank:** Maggie doesn't dodge. **Core moves:** *Set* (roots in place, density up: damage taken drops 80%, enemies who melee her stagger themselves), *Beam Walk* (a slow unstoppable advance that shoves the whole enemy front line back — territory control as a punch), *Spotter's Call* (taunts everything in a cone; the squad takes zero damage from taunted enemies for 4 seconds). **Signature — "Topping Out":** maximum density, one jump, one landing. The shockwave knocks down everything, including car alarms three blocks over, which the gossip system files under "Ballast again."
- **Personality:** unshockable; generous with time, stingy with sympathy ("you'll live"); the driest one-liners in the roster, delivered at load-bearing volume.
- **Day job / hangout:** union ironworker, Local 512 — currently on the Hennepin corridor build. After shift: **Moe's** bar, back booth, one beer, the crossword, in pen.
- **Recruitment:** work. She won't join a squad she hasn't stood a shift with. Take three faction jobs alongside her as a non-recruit ally first; if you cut corners on any of them, the third job never comes.
- **Romance/friendship hook:** **Friendship-only, and the richest arc for it.** Maggie's wife died four winters ago — the first-snow event lands on their anniversary, and Maggie's scene that night is the reason the friendship track exists. She is Ojibwe, White Earth descent, raised half in the city and half up north; she will feed you, insult you, teach you to fix drywall, and tell you exactly which of your romantic decisions is stupid, in order. Everyone in the roster is a little in love with her. She knows. It's not like that.
- **Is-this-boring test:** she walked out of the building collapse that killed two coworkers in '19 by holding a floor up for six minutes, and has attended both families' Christmases every year since. She has never once called herself a hero and gets quietly, dangerously angry when the press does.

### 7. Yusuf Ali — "MERIDIAN"
- **Age:** 30.
- **Power kit — Telekinetic (precision, not poltergeist):** Yusuf is a physical therapist; his TK works like his hands — exact. **Core moves:** *Pin* (locks one enemy's limb mid-swing; timing turns hits into whiffs), *Traction* (drags an enemy out of formation to the squad — the pick tool), *Alignment* (a squad buff: everyone's next attack auto-crits because he *nudged* it two degrees true). **Signature — "Manipulation Under Anesthesia":** briefly puppets the biggest enemy on the field as an ally. He apologizes to them afterward. Every time.
- **Personality:** courteous in a way that makes rude people feel it; principled and unbothered about the cost; a chess hustler's patience — he's always three moves in, even in small talk.
- **Day job / hangout:** physical therapist at a Lyn-Lake clinic; half his clients are uninsured and billed in ambiguity. Found at **Farhia's Halal Market** (his aunt's — see Independents) restocking shelves he doesn't work at, or playing speed chess at the lake tables, taking money off tourists politely.
- **Recruitment:** through the community, not to him. Yusuf joins when the Commons faction (see Bee Toliver) vouches for you at rep tier 3 — he trusts Bee's read on people over his own, which is itself a story he'll only tell you at friendship max.
- **Romance/friendship hook:** *Open, Exclusive, demisexual slow burn* — Yusuf doesn't do sparks, he does foundations; his romance track has the fewest flirt options and the most weight per line. Baggage: an almost-engagement two years ago that his family still mourns louder than he does, and a brother in Nairobi he sends money to and lies to about how.
- **Is-this-boring test:** he can feel the exact weight of anything he looks at — and once held a collapsing pedestrian bridge for nine minutes with 62 people on it, then went home before the news arrived. August is the only person who knows, because August heard his heartbeat that day: dead calm.

### 8. Camille Dubois — "KILN"
- **Age:** 33.
- **Power kit — Fire/heat (craft, not carnage):** Camille shapes heat like glass. **Core moves:** *Glasswork* (pulls molten barriers out of the air — cover on demand that enemies burn on), *Anneal* (a heat-wrap on an ally that converts 50% of damage they take into a delayed heal — the game's weirdest, best support tool), *Flashpoint* (detonates any of her cooling glass structures). **Signature — "Studio Fire":** an expanding ring of shimmering heat-haze; enemies inside take ramping burn and — the flourish — their weapons visibly droop and slag.
- **Personality:** loud, precise, and warm, in that order; flirts as punctuation; New Orleans to the bone and openly at war with Minnesota winter ("y'all *chose* this?").
- **Day job / hangout:** glassblower with a studio garage off 26th, sells at craft fairs, teaches a Thursday class that has a waitlist because of the arms, and she knows it. Post-studio: **The Velvet Antler**, where Cass Delane comps her drinks and she lets him think that means something.
- **Recruitment:** her studio's lease is a pawn in the Odegaard–Isles Trust land fight (see Factions). Secure the building — buy it, leverage it, or burn the right document (her suggestion, delivered while making direct eye contact and a very thin wine glass).
- **Romance/friendship hook:** *Open, non-exclusive and honest about it* — Camille dates like a group show, not a solo exhibition, and she'll tell you on date one; jealous players get a kind, unmovable speech. Get close and the volume drops: she left New Orleans after the flood took the family studio, and every piece she blows is one her grandmother taught her, from memory, in order. She's on piece 214 of an unknown total.
- **Is-this-boring test:** she cannot be burned but can be cold, and Minnesota winter is the first thing in her life that's ever been genuinely dangerous to her. Her first-snow-event scene — furious, laughing, wrapped in four coats, making the snow steam around her — is the roster's best comedy beat with teeth.

### 9. Dominic Reyes — "SLIP"
- **Age:** 28.
- **Power kit — Phasing (the un-wall):** Dom stops being solid; matter files a complaint. **Core moves:** *Ghost Step* (phase through an attack with a counter-window on re-solidify), *Doorman* (phases a SECTION OF WALL for 3 seconds — the whole squad flanks through architecture), *Pickpocket* (phases a hand into an enemy: steals the item, the weapon, or — on brutes — a satisfying *click* that disables armor). **Signature — "Half In":** re-solidifies with an arm inside a wall, floor, or vehicle and *anchors* the fight's biggest problem to it. Comic-splash-page rule: this move always frames well and he always checks his hair after.
- **Personality:** charm as a locksmith's tool — every conversation, he's feeling for pins; vain, and funny about it; ethical in a code entirely of his own drafting.
- **Day job / hangout:** locksmith and escape-room designer ("both directions of the same skill"). Shop on Hennepin, but he takes his coffee across the street at the record store to flirt with the clientele in reflection off the shop window.
- **Recruitment:** he's being blackmailed. The Isles Trust has proof of his old career — three years of extremely artistic burglary, Isles mansions exclusively — and they want him back on the payroll. Get the leverage off him. How you do it (steal it back with him, buy it, trade it) sets his whole arc's temperature.
- **Romance/friendship hook:** *Oriented (men), jealous in denial* — Dom performs cool detachment and then phases through a wall "coincidentally" whenever your dates walk past his shop; the gossip system rats him out and it's delicious. Baggage: the burglary years had a partner, and the partner didn't retire, and every so often a mansion job in the papers has Dom's old signature move in it, done sloppier.
- **Is-this-boring test:** he has never in his life been locked in, out, or up — and is more claustrophobic than any character in the game. Not of spaces. Of situations. The romance arc is watching a man who can walk through walls learn to stay in a room.

### 10. Priya Chandrasekhar — "BROWNOUT"
- **Age:** 35.
- **Power kit — Electricity (the grid made flesh):** utility-scale, utility-tempered. **Core moves:** *Arc Bite* (a chaining jolt that jumps to enemies who are touching or adjacent — punishes formations), *Load Shed* (drains charge from anything electrical nearby into a shield — in Uptown, that's every fight), *Live Wire* (drops a crackling line on the ground; enemies crossing it lock up mid-step). **Signature — "Rolling Blackout":** kills every light on the block for five seconds — enemies blind, her squad sees by her glow, and the neighborhood files it under "Priya's having a week."
- **Personality:** blunt as a fuse box; competence-arrogant and earned; secretly sentimental and would rather be electrocuted than caught at it.
- **Day job / hangout:** journeyman electrician, half her jobs off-book for neighbors who can't afford code. Drinks one bourbon at Moe's on Fridays, fixes his neon sign every time, refuses payment, insults the wiring.
- **Recruitment:** money and respect, in that order. She's the only hero with a straightforward hiring negotiation — and lowballing her locks the recruit for a full season. She has a rate. The rate is the personality test.
- **Romance/friendship hook:** *Open, Exclusive — the most jealous partner in the game, and the most honest about it:* "I notice everything with a current in it, and you, sweetheart, run about ninety millivolts." Divorced; her ex-husband got the house, the dog, and the version of her that apologized. Get close and you find the sentimental core: she keeps every broken thing she's ever fixed for someone who didn't come back.
- **Is-this-boring test:** she can feel the whole neighborhood's power draw like a pulse — she knows who's home, who left the stove on, whose grow-op is whose — and treats that omniscience with a lineworker's total professional discretion. The one time she breaks that code is a story arc, and it's because someone's pattern looked like her own worst year.

## 2. Faction Leaders (4 factions, one neighborhood, not enough neighborhood)

Uptown's turf is a four-way cold war. Every faction offers real value; every faction costs you standing with at least one other. Rep is earned in public — the gossip system is the rep system's nervous system.

### CASS DELANE — The Velvet Antler (nightlife)
- **Profile:** 44. Owner of **The Velvet Antler** — taxidermy-chic club, magenta light, a VIP mezzanine everyone claims to hate and fights to get into. Cass is seduction as infrastructure: he remembers your drink, your ex, and your price, and quotes all three back at the exact wrong moment. Charming like an undertow. **Personality:** patient, theatrical, transactional to the marrow — but pays debts to the penny, both kinds. **The dangerous part:** the Antler's back book runs card games, a supers-only speakeasy below the cooler, and half of Uptown's smuggled goods. Cass has never thrown a punch; people volunteer.
- **Offers the player:** club jobs (bouncing, debt collection with style clauses, "escort this idiot home alive"), the best fence/vendor in the game, after-dark intel on every other faction, and **followers:** slick brawler "door staff" units for the base. Romance-adjacent: Cass will flirt like a five-alarm fire, and the arc is discovering whether there's a person under the proprietor. (There is. He hates it.)
- **Rep rises:** completing night jobs, spending big, keeping his secrets, being interesting. **Rep falls:** violence inside the club (bad for business), talking to cops or Odegaard's office, being boring — genuinely, repeated dull dialogue choices tick rep down. Cass's respect is a bandwidth issue.
- **Conflicts:** Odegaard shakes him down via inspections; Bee Toliver considers him a landlord of vices; the Isles Trust launders through him and he's one audit from owned.

### BERNICE "BEE" TOLIVER — The Commons (the neighbors)
- **Profile:** 61. Runs the **Lyndale Commons co-op and garden network** — and, informally, the phone tree that can put forty neighbors on a corner in eleven minutes, several of them retired supers who "don't do that anymore" until they do. Bee marched here in the 60s as a child on her mother's hip and has outlasted every developer since. **Personality:** warm like a kitchen, ruthless like a chess clock; keeps receipts going back decades; her hugs and her ultimatums use the same arms. **The militia:** the Casserole Brigade is a joke name the neighbors use in public and absolutely not a joke.
- **Offers the player:** the farming system's best seeds and crop contracts, cheap base food/healing vendors, recruitment access (Yusuf gates behind her), and **followers:** neighbor-watch units — individually weak, endless morale, free upkeep because they're fed by the garden your crops feed.
- **Rep rises:** garden work, defending shops, showing up TWICE (Bee's rule: anyone can show up once), giving generously in public. **Rep falls:** property damage in residential blocks, working for Odegaard, romancing-and-ghosting a neighbor (the gossip hits Bee's phone tree in hours).
- **Conflicts:** existential war with Odegaard (rezoning) and the Isles Trust (buying duplexes for cash); complicated truce with Cass — he hires her kids' summer jobs and she pretends not to know what the cooler hides.

### COUNCILMEMBER GRANT ODEGAARD — The Ward Office (the machine)
- **Profile:** 52. Fourth-term councilmember, smile like a ribbon-cutting, handshake like a subpoena. Odegaard is corruption in its most Minnesotan form: nothing so vulgar as a bribe — just permits that stall, inspections that materialize, and a nonprofit run by his brother-in-law. **Personality:** passive-aggression as a martial art; genuinely believes he IS the neighborhood; never angry, only "disappointed, frankly." **His capes:** **Civic Shield LLC**, a private super-security firm on city retainer — matching windbreakers, body cameras that malfunction on schedule.
- **Offers the player:** the highest-paying jobs in the game (with the dirtiest fine print), permit fast-tracking for base upgrades that other factions gate, legal cover after messy fights, and **followers:** Civic Shield contractor units — the strongest hireables, and they unionize against YOU at low loyalty, which is the joke and the design.
- **Rep rises:** doing his cleanup work, photo ops, delivering dirt on other factions. **Rep falls:** public embarrassments, siding with Bee at council meetings, any recorded contradiction of his version of events. Uniquely: his rep NEVER fully decays — Odegaard forgives nothing and forgets nothing, he just re-prices you.
- **Conflicts:** squeezing Cass for a cut of the Antler; rezoning Bee out of existence; publicly at war with the Isles Trust and privately their errand boy, which is the campaign's best-kept open secret.

### ADELAIDE WRAY — The Isles Trust (old money, cold water)
- **Profile:** 70. Matriarch of a flour-fortune family whose name is on two museums and zero indictments. Runs the **Isles Trust** from a Lake of the Isles mansion with staff, hedges, and a dock nobody swims from. **Personality:** courteous like a blade in a velvet box; finds open ambition vulgar and open emotion worse; collects people the way the family collected art — provenance mattering more than beauty. **The syndicate:** the Trust owns paper — mortgages, liens, blackmail (see: Dominic), city debt — and hires talent only through three layers of lawyers. Adelaide's one visible vice: she attends every funeral in Uptown, invited or not, and no one has ever had the nerve to ask why.
- **Offers the player:** capital — the only faction that funds base expansion outright; rare-goods auctions; society access (the mansion parties are a whole flirtation economy in tuxedos); and **followers:** discreet "estate security" units — few, elite, and they report on you to her, always, forever.
- **Rep rises:** discretion, profitable partnerships, favors done without being told twice, defeating people she dislikes *quietly*. **Rep falls:** publicity of any kind involving her name, sentimentality in negotiations, and — steepest penalty in the game — telling anyone what she's asked of you.
- **Conflicts:** buying the ground out from under Bee block by block; holds Odegaard's leash and lets him bark at her in public for appearances; regards Cass as "the help who forgot" — she sold him the Antler's building and kept the mineral rights to his soul.

## 3. Notable Independents (9 named NPCs, faction-free, opinion-rich)

- **MOE OKONKWO, 57 — owner-bartender, Moe's.** A dive with taxidermy older than Cass's whole aesthetic. Moe pours slow, listens fast, and has a strict "no capes on shift" rule he applies only to people he likes. *Schedule:* behind the bar 4pm–2am, Tuesdays off (nobody knows where he goes; the gossip system tracks seven theories, all wrong). *Remembers/gossips:* who paid their tab, who cried in the back booth, who you left with. *Romance:* no — happily, mysteriously married to someone no one's ever seen.
- **BIG RONDA, 49 — owner, The Nite Owl diner.** Open 24 hours; Ronda is the 2am confessor of Uptown and prices comfort accordingly. Feeds Theo free "because he's underweight for his speed class," a thing she invented. *Schedule:* the counter, 10pm–8am. *Remembers/gossips:* every after-fight meal, every morning-after breakfast, seated apart or together. *Romance:* no; she'll comment on yours like a sports analyst.
- **ANDRE "DRE" SIMMONS, 36 — hairdresser, Crown & Anchor salon.** THE gossip hub — the node with the highest spread multiplier in the game, and he knows it, and he curates. Cuts half the roster's hair, including Cass's, under an ironclad neutrality he calls "the chair is Switzerland." *Schedule:* salon Tues–Sat; Antler on Saturdays after close. *Romance:* eligible (men), high standards, will still do your hair after he dumps you because he's a professional.
- **KAI BERGSTRÖM, 27 — lifeguard, Bde Maka Ska main beach.** Sunny, jacked, disarmingly well-read — the paperback in the lifeguard chair rotates through Russian novelists. Half of Uptown fakes drowning annually. *Schedule:* beach 9–7 in summer; the arena (learning to skate, badly, near Ingrid) after the first snow. *Remembers/gossips:* everything that happens on the shoreline — which, since the running path is Uptown's artery, is everything. *Romance:* eligible, any player; refreshingly free of baggage, which unsettles the whole roster.
- **WREN ITO, 41 — tattoo artist, Second Skin.** Books out four months; does every super's ink and keeps their secrets under needle-confidentiality. Speaks in un-asked-for wisdom that's right about 70% of the time — she tracks her own average. *Schedule:* studio noon–8, walk-ins never, exceptions for good stories. *Remembers/gossips:* remembers everything, gossips NEVER — the one node where information goes to die, which makes her invaluable and interrogated constantly. *Romance:* eligible (any), but only for players with high faction-independence — Wren doesn't date joiners.
- **PETRA LIND, 36 — yoga studio owner; Ingrid's ex-wife.** Kind, evolved, insufferable about both. The divorce was amicable, which everyone agrees is the worst kind. Her studio faces the arena. It didn't used to. *Schedule:* studio 6am–2pm; the lake path at sunset, running past the arena's glass at the exact hour the Zamboni runs. *Remembers/gossips:* frames all gossip as "concern," a mechanic in itself — Petra-sourced gossip arrives softened and lands worse. *Romance:* eligible (women) — and doing so drags the Ingrid arc sideways in ways the game absolutely intends.
- **FARHIA HASSAN, 63 — owner, Farhia's Halal Market; Yusuf's aunt.** Runs the block's true bank of favors. Extends credit on gut feeling, has never been wrong, and audibly narrates her assessment of you to no one while weighing your produce. *Schedule:* market 7am–9pm, mosque Fridays; her stoop with tea when the weather permits, which in Minnesota is a threat. *Remembers/gossips:* debts of all kinds; the market ledger is the neighborhood's real credit score. *Romance:* no, and she will be personally managing whether you're good enough for her nephew.
- **OTIS GRANT, 68 — clerk (and secret owner), Vinyl Lake Records.** Pretends to be the employee so he can insult customers without "management" consequences. Encyclopedic on Minneapolis music; if you earn side one of the good stories, you get funk anecdotes that would end careers. *Schedule:* store 11–7; The Foundry on funk nights, where August comps him the good booth. *Remembers/gossips:* forgets your name for four visits, then remembers your entire purchase history forever — the promotion system in one man. *Romance:* no; will judge everyone you date by their record collection, out loud.
- **MARCUS DEAN, 52 — driver, the 21 bus.** Sees Uptown eight times a day at window height. Greets regulars by stop, not name. ## 4. The NPC Archetype System (making 100 strangers feel like people)

The ~80–120 ambient NPCs are built from **archetype templates × personality dials × two quirks.** The goal: any NPC the player squints at should survive the squint.

### Dials (0–100, rolled per NPC within archetype ranges)
- **WARMTH** — default goodwill. Governs greeting tier, forgiveness rate, gift reactions.
- **BOLDNESS** — will they approach the player, flirt first, run toward or from a fight.
- **CHATTINESS** — line length, gossip volume, interrupt frequency.
- **MISCHIEF** — lie rate, tease rate, whether their gossip mutates (see §6).

### Archetype templates (name / dial ranges / voice note)
| Archetype | Warmth | Boldness | Chat | Mischief | Voice note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Young Professional | 40–70 | 30–60 | 40–70 | 20–40 | Optimizes everything, including this conversation. |
| Punk | 30–60 | 60–90 | 30–70 | 60–90 | Aggressively kind under aggressive everything else. |
| Retiree | 50–90 | 40–80 | 60–95 | 30–70 | Has time, uses it on you; passive-aggression fluent. |
| Jogger | 40–70 | 50–80 | 20–50 | 10–30 | Talks in intervals; gossip delivered mid-stride. |
| Barfly | 30–70 | 40–70 | 70–95 | 50–80 | Moe's Greek chorus; memory excellent before 10pm. |
| Student | 40–75 | 20–70 | 50–85 | 40–75 | Everything is either "so real" or a crisis; no middle. |
| Co-op Parent | 45–80 | 30–60 | 55–90 | 20–50 | Weaponized "just wondering"; Bee's info militia. |
| Service Lifer | 35–70 | 40–75 | 30–65 | 40–70 | Seen everything twice; tips define their whole read of you. |
| Lake Rat | 50–85 | 55–85 | 40–75 | 30–60 | Shirtless in April; the shoreline's ambient witnesses. |

| Gutter Poet | 30–65 | 60–95 | 20–90 | 55–95 | Busker/oracle; occasionally, accidentally, correct. |

Each ambient NPC also rolls **two quirks** from a pool (~60 entries: "always eating something," "quotes their therapist," "hates one specific hero on principle," "calls everyone 'chief,'" "visibly rooting for your worst romance option"...). Dials + quirks + archetype voice = no two strangers read identical, at template cost.

### Promotion: from ambient to named ("the sim remembers them")
- **Track:** every ambient NPC keeps a lightweight interaction count and a flag for high-impact events (you saved them, fought beside/against them, flirted, insulted, gifted, stood near one of your fade-to-blacks).
- **Trigger:** 3 meaningful interactions OR 1 high-impact event → **promotion.** The NPC receives: a generated name (culturally coherent with their model), a home anchor, a schedule (two anchors + commute), one relationship link to an existing named NPC ("Dre's cousin," "rides Marcus's bus"), and **one secret** rolled from a table — the secret is what makes them worth a second conversation.
- **Recognition dialogue flips:** post-promotion they reference the promoting event forever ("You're the one who caught the awning. I tell people that.").
- **Caps:** max ~15 player-promoted NPCs live at once; the least-interacted demote gracefully (they move, cite a reason, gossip about it once).
- **The payoff rule:** any promoted NPC can later surface in content — as a gossip source, a date's plus-one, a witness at a faction hearing. Promotion is the sim proving pillar 1: *nothing is set dressing.*

## 5. Memory & Opinion — The Voice Bible

Opinion is a scale: **GRUDGE → WARY → NEUTRAL → WARM → CLOSE → SMITTEN.** Every line the dialogue system assembles carries (a) opinion tier, (b) up to two memory tags, (c) the speaker's dials. Rules for writers: memory references are *specific, never summarized* ("you caught the awning," never "you helped me"); higher opinion buys longer sentences and worse jokes; GRUDGE tier is short, cold, and never cartoonish; flirtation only generates at WARM+ AND player-initiated history (tone bible: opt-in, always).

**Ten calibration lines** (the system's target voice — writers match these):

1. **[GRUDGE + saw_you_fight_for_odegaard]** — "Ope — no, go ahead. Sidewalk's yours. Everything else apparently is too."
2. **[WARY + you_flirted_with_their_friend]** — "Nia says hi. She does the thing with the eyebrow when she says it. That's all I'm saying."
3. **[NEUTRAL + no_tags, high CHAT retiree]** — "Cold enough for ya? I'm kidding, it's August. Ask me in October and mean it."
4. **[WARM + you_saved_their_shop]** — "Register's short a coffee. Been short one every morning since the window thing. Take the coffee, hero, don't make it weird."
5. **[WARM + saw_you_lose_a_fight]** — "Saw you eat pavement on Lagoon Tuesday. Stood back up nice, though. That's the part I told people."
6. **[CLOSE + you_stood_them_up]** — "I got the booth. I got the good booth. I sat in the good booth for fifty minutes, and Ronda gave me pie and did NOT ask questions, which is how I know it looked as bad as it felt. You get one of those. That was it."
7. **[CLOSE + heard_gossip_your_romance_ended]** — "Dre didn't tell me anything. Dre told me everything. You want to get pancakes and say none of it out loud?"
8. **[SMITTEN + they_watched_you_win + first_snow]** — "You were showing off. Rolling Blackout on a Tuesday, please — the whole block went dark and all I could see was you glowing. So. My place has candles."
9. **[SMITTEN→GRUDGE pivot + caught_you_cheating, exclusive partner]** — "I fix things for a living, so believe me on the diagnosis: this isn't tripped, sweetheart. It's burnt out." *(Priya — exclusive-partner confrontations are hand-written per hero, never templated.)*
10. **[WARY + witnessed_your_generosity + high MISCHIEF punk]** — "Saw you cover that guy's tab like nobody was watching. Somebody's always watching. It's usually me. You're fine, probably. Don't ruin it."

## 6. Gossip Rules (the city's nervous system)

**What spreads, ranked by juice (spread speed multiplier):**
1. **Romance & betrayal (×3):** who left with whom, who got caught, who cried at the Nite Owl. Fade-to-blacks are private; *arrivals and departures are not.*
2. **Fights (×2.5):** wins travel fast; LOSSES travel faster with better details. Property damage adds Bee's phone tree to the route.
3. **Betrayals of factions (×2.5):** flipping on a faction is permanent-record gossip; it never fully decays, it re-prices (Odegaard rule, city-wide).
4. **Generosity (×1.5, slow but sticky):** kindness spreads slower, decays slowest, and compounds — three separate generosity rumors merge into a reputation trait ("good tipper energy").
5. **Weirdness (×1):** schedule anomalies, Isles trips at 3am, whatever Ballast's car-alarm shockwave was this time.

**Mechanics:** gossip hops person-to-person along real schedule overlaps — no broadcast. **Hubs** (Dre ×3, Moe ×2, Marcus's bus ×2, the salon chairs, the lake path) multiply. High-MISCHIEF carriers *mutate* details one notch per hop (a lost fight becomes a lost fight *and a torn shirt*). Wren is a sink: gossip entering Second Skin dies. Petra-type "concern framing" softens wording, worsens reception. Typical juicy item saturates Uptown in ~2 in-game days; hits your partner in hours if any hop touches a hub.

**Five example chains:**
1. **The Awning:** You catch a falling awning over two brunchers → Jogger (saw it) → lake path → Kai (lifeguard chair, adds "one-handed," true) → Dre's Saturday chair → by Monday, Cass comps your first drink "for the show." *Generosity + weirdness, ×1.5, fully saturated in 3 days, unlocks a promotion candidate: one of the brunchers.*
2. **The Good Booth:** You stand up your CLOSE-tier date at the Nite Owl → Ronda (saw the pie hour) tells no one but *seats you apart* forever, which the Barflies read like a headline → Moe hears the seating chart changed → your date's friends go WARY across the board in 36 hours. *No one gossiped, exactly. The room did.*
3. **The Windbreaker Job:** You quietly do a cleanup job for Odegaard → a Civic Shield contractor's bodycam "malfunctions" but the Co-op Parent across the street doesn't → Bee's phone tree by dinner → Commons rep −2 tiers before you've been paid → Bee's next greeting is calibration line #1's colder cousin.
4. **The Two-Timers' Waltz:** You're exclusive with Marisol and get seen leaving the Antler at 3am with a dancer's jacket over your shoulders → Barfly → Moe (says nothing, pours Marisol's usual *without being asked*, which she clocks instantly) → Marisol confronts you mid-derby-practice, publicly, on skates. Mutation risk: if the chain passes a high-MISCHIEF punk, the jacket becomes "her jacket AND his helmet," and now you're fighting two rumors, one of them false, and the false one is stickier.
5. **The Funeral Guest:** You spot Adelaide Wray at a stranger's funeral and tell one person → the chain runs TOWARD the Isles for once → dies abruptly at every hop adjacent to Trust money → Wren, of all people, mentions she's heard it — meaning it crossed the whole city underground → an estate-security unit starts appearing in your base's follower rotation *unrequested.* Some gossip is a message you sent without knowing. Adelaide answers all her mail.
