# 04 — Characters: The People of Twin Cities

**Contract:** Every person in this doc obeys `01-vision.md` — pillar 4 ("people, not vending machines") and the tone bible (R-rated energy, fade-to-black discipline, hard lines absolute). Every character below was written to pass one test: *would a TV writer keep this person in the pilot?* If a character ever reads as a quest dispenser, rewrite them or cut them.

---

## 1. The Anchor & The Hero Roster (the PC + 10 recruitable supers)

**Player identity (binding — supersedes all earlier rulings):** The player character IS **the Anchor — Elias "Eli" Monroe (§1.0)**: single dad, the only known multithreader, carrier of the buried Act (`09-story-lore.md` is the spoiler canon). Romance, reputation, dialogue identity, opinion tiers, and gossip all attach to him. All 10 roster heroes are **recruitable** (6 in the slice) — the squad is the Anchor + 2 recruits — and **romanceable per their existing flags.** Deeds done by squadmates credit the squad; personal credit — and personal consequences — land on Eli. Unrecruited heroes don't vanish: they keep their schedules, their opinions, and their feelings about not getting picked.

**Vertical slice (binding):** the slice ships **6 recruitable heroes** — Marisol, August, Ingrid, Theo, Maggie, Camille — chosen for role spread (brawler / ranged control / control / mobility / tank / zone-support) and romance-style spread (exclusive-jealous, slow-burn, oriented-exclusive, skittish-open, friendship-only, non-exclusive). The other four are marked **POST-SLICE** in place, with the reason stated.

**Control contract (binding, per hero):** each hero fields ONE equipped core move by default; the other two unlock at **Signature-Evolution tree nodes**; loadout is swappable at base. Signatures double as finishers (see §1.11). **Design note — no healer role by design:** Camille's Anneal is the only heal in the game and it's a *conversion*, not a pool. **Squad-viability flag:** all-melee squads (slice example: Marisol+Theo+Maggie; full-roster example: Marisol+Theo+Dom) vs dual-ranged enemy waves is a known tuning corner — combat doc owns the fix.

**Romance legend (applied to a fixed PC):** *Open* = romanceable by Eli. *Oriented* = the character's stated orientation is real and stays real: Dominic (men) is romanceable; Ingrid and Petra (women) are **not into you, and that's content** — their arcs (including the protected Ingrid–Petra geometry) run at full richness on the friendship/ally track, per the tone bible's "some NPCs just aren't into you." *Exclusive* = demands monogamy; the gossip system WILL get you caught. *Friendship-only* = not romanceable, and richer for it.

### 1.0 THE ANCHOR — Elias "Eli" Monroe (the player character)
- **Age:** 39. Single dad; Lucía's father (§1.0a); Renata's ex (§1.0b). Full spoiler canon: `09-story-lore.md` §5.
- **Power kit — Multithread (the only one on Earth):** everyone else carries one power; Eli *holds threads* — sustained echo-resonances that **keep running while he acts.** On the POWER button, a thread is a program, not a spell: light it and it persists (a running *Black Ice* echo under his feet while he brawls with a *Payback* echo in his hands). **Threads come from attunement:** a bonded squadmate consents to let him read their resonance, unlocking an echo of one of their core moves — deeper bond, higher-fidelity echo (relationship systems feed combat; pillar 3). **Signature/finisher — "Full Hands":** every equipped thread runs at once for a few seconds; the screen fills with everyone he's let in. **Upgrade tree (binding):** thread COUNT (1 → 2 → 3...) and thread CLASS (which move types may run on a thread: Stance, Field, Strike echoes). **Slot Zero (binding UI storytelling):** his first slot is permanently occupied and permanently lit — Lucía's unset resonance, held since the Act, unspendable, never droppable. Players will ask what the locked glowing slot is for hours before the story answers. Loadout swappable at base, per the control contract.
- **Personality (written for a player-avatar — strong silhouette, room for choice):** steady under load — the man you want holding the door; dry Minnesota deflection as a first language; and a guilt he has built an entire functioning life around, which the game slowly makes load-bearing. Dialogue tones (warm / wry / hard) all read true to him; what never changes is that he shows up, he counts exits, and he lies only by omission — the specific omission being the plot.
- **Day job / hangout:** runs the base (property management, officially); school pickup line at 2:50; Moe's when Lucía's at her mom's — where the "no capes on shift" rule suits him better than anyone, because he's the one cape who's grateful for a room where nobody asks what he can do.
- **Romance:** he's the player — every flag in this doc points at him. What's authored on his side: exes exist, the co-parenting calendar is real, and Lucía has a rubric (§1.0a). Fade-to-black discipline absolute, per 01.
- **Is-this-boring test:** the most studied, most wanted, most feared person in the Cities reads, at the school potluck, as a quiet dad with good hotdish and tired eyes — and both things are completely true. The game is about which one the city decides to believe, stage by stage.

### 1.0a THE KID — Lucía Monroe-Vásquez
- **Age:** 9. Bi-racial (Eli + the Vásquez family); her maternal family is rooted in the **Lake Street corridor** — abuela Imelda's panadería (§3B) is the center of her Saturdays, which makes the Rogue Zone a custody-calendar catastrophe before it's ever politics. **Never in combat, never harmed on screen — binding.** The threat to her is structural: a perimeter between her and half her family.
- **Wants:** a dog (active lobbying campaign, laminated slides, cost projections in marker); to bike the Greenway alone ("Theo does it" is not, she has been told, an argument); her Saturdays back.
- **Opinions (the dating-sim friction gold):** she rates Eli's dates on a hidden five-star system she calls *the rubric* and files reports with her mother. Current standings — **Maggie: her favorite person alive** (cribbage, no baby-talk, taught her the word "load-bearing"), and Maggie's friendship-only track is half Lucía content by volume. **Camille:** adored (glass beads, glitter, treats her like an apprentice with standards). **Ingrid:** approached with awed FORMALITY (a legend from the arena; Lucía skates better every winter to be worth talking to). **August:** made her a falling-asleep mix once; permanent status. **Theo:** considered a peer (correct). She is deeply suspicious of any adult who is nice to her *strategically* — "you're using me as a level" — and her rubric leaks to the gossip graph through school, mutation-heavy.
- **Her own mini-track:** squad heroes accrue Lucía-approval separately from Eli's bonds; her approval unlocks scenes (she is a gate to warmth, never a veto on romance — she's a kid, not a mechanic). All Lucía content is strictly non-romantic in every direction, hard line, obviously.
- **What she knows/suspects about the Act:** she knows dad "holds her power for her" — coat-check, she's always been told, hers when she's grown. She has started asking *when.* And she has noticed two things she hasn't told anyone: the robots know her cousins' names, and her dad can't look at them while they recite.

### 1.0b THE EX — Renata Vásquez
- **Age:** 36. Pediatric ICU nurse (the irony is load-bearing and never joked about). Co-parents Lucía on alternating weeks with a competence that makes new partners on both sides nervous. Her family's panadería sits inside the Rogue Zone perimeter; her brother Tomás is flagged (§3B). **Not a villain. Not fridged. Alive, busy, and right about most things.**
- **History with Eli:** eight years. The ice (`09` §5) didn't end them — she went in after Lucía and carries that afternoon in her lungs and hands, and nobody in this game will ever blame her for thirty seconds. What ended them was the *silence after*: she co-signed the Paradigm consent, knew about the study, and never got the rest. **The rest is two secrets on two schedules (`09` §5):** she learns the registry *exists, and who wrote it,* at **Stage 3 — with the whole city, from the news, unless Eli pre-empts by one scene** (told by him vs told by the news: same fact, different marriage-shaped crater; both versions authored, out-of-system). She gets the **full ledger** — the eighteen-month walk, the anonymization clause — only at **Stage 5**, her second authored scene. She may know more than the player has admitted and less than the player fears, and every handoff between Stages 3 and 5 is written under that pressure.
- **One confident wrongness** (never the thirty seconds — the game doesn't touch that): Renata is calmly, immovably certain that Lucía's rubric is a phase. It is not a phase. Lucía has a succession plan for the rubric.
- **Texture for the dating sim:** she has opinions about who Eli dates, delivered deadpan at handoffs ("The glassblower. Huh. Lucía says four stars. Steep rubric this quarter."), and the kid reports in both directions with the fidelity of a court stenographer. Renata is dating Ben, an orthopedic surgeon of catastrophic niceness (Lucía's rating: two stars, "no upside"). **No romance track in the slice** — one authored "what are we" scene exists on the far side of the main arc, in both directions, and the game promises nothing.

### 1. Marisol Vega — "REBOUND" *(SLICE)*
- **Age:** 31.
- **Power kit — Kinetic brawler (momentum theft):** She doesn't take hits, she *banks* them. **Core moves:** *Absorb Stance* (soak an incoming hit into a stored-momentum meter), *Payback Palm* (dump stored force into one open-hand shot), *Wallride* (converts her own movement into a ricochet dash off any surface). **Signature/finisher — "Interest":** stores three consecutive hits without attacking, then returns all of it at 150% in a single screen-shaking counter. High risk, highest single-hit number in the game.
- **Control contract:** Equipped default: *Absorb Stance*; *Payback Palm*/*Wallride* unlock at Signature-Evolution tree nodes; loadout swappable at base.
- **Personality:** competitive to the point of self-sabotage; loyal like a dog with a grudge; laughs loudest at her own worst decisions.
- **Day job / hangout:** bike courier and part-time mechanic at **Spoke & Dagger** on Lagoon. Off hours: roller derby practice, or the last stool at Moe's bar arguing about whose fault the derby loss was (hers).
- **Recruitment:** beat her. Not in a mission — she challenges the player to a 1v1 in the Spoke & Dagger alley after you break up a fight she was *winning*. You don't have to win, but you have to land the last hit clean. She respects the receipt.
- **Romance/friendship hook:** *Open, Exclusive-jealous.* Marisol dates like she fights — all in, no guard. Her last three relationships ended when she caught feelings faster than the other person and torched it preemptively. Get close and you learn she keeps her late brother's courier bag on the wall, unwashed, and the derby isn't a hobby, it's the only place she lets herself lose.
- **Is-this-boring test:** she can *feel* the stored kinetic energy of everyone around her — she knows who's about to throw a punch, who's about to cry, and who's about to kiss her, three seconds early. She still gets all three wrong constantly. **Mechanical (binding):** enemy telegraphs display **0.5s earlier** for Marisol than for any other hero; in dialogue she gets unique **interrupt-before-the-line** options — cutting an NPC off before they finish — which read as uncanny at high bond and *misfire embarrassingly at low bond*.

### 2. Frankie Larsen — "MOONDOG" *(POST-SLICE: gravity's 3D repositioning needs combat-camera work scheduled after the slice.)*
- **Age:** 26. Nonbinary (they/them).
- **Power kit — Gravity control (local weight editing):** Frankie makes things heavy or light in a bubble. **Core moves:** *Featherfield* (allies in the zone jump triple-height, fall slow — repositioning god), *Anvil Zone* (enemies in the zone drop to their knees, attack speed halved), *Sling* (grab any prop, gravity-yeet it as a projectile arc). **Signature/finisher — "Perigee":** collapses both zones into one point — everything airborne slams down at once for a crowd-clear crater. **Binding differentiation:** Perigee deals **0 damage to grounded targets** — launch synergy required; Frankie's whole kit is the setup. Fights around Frankie happen in three dimensions; nobody else's do.
- **Control contract:** Equipped default: *Featherfield*; *Anvil Zone*/*Sling* unlock at Signature-Evolution tree nodes; loadout swappable at base.
- **Personality:** serene until they're absolutely not; allergic to plans; treats danger like weather — noted, then ignored.
- **Day job / hangout:** aerialist and instructor at **Sideshow North**, a circus-arts warehouse off the Greenway. Found on the trapeze rig at closing time, upside down, holding a conversation like it's normal.
- **Recruitment:** their rig gets condemned by one of the Aldermen's inspectors (see Factions). Save the warehouse — through money, leverage on the inspector, or one very public demonstration of what "structurally unsound" looks like when a gravity-controller is annoyed.
- **Romance/friendship hook:** *Open, non-exclusive — and Frankie's non-exclusivity is ABSOLUTE:* they never generate jealousy events, ever, in either direction; it is a settled fact of them, not a negotiation (contrast Camille, §1.8). Dating Frankie WHILE dating an Exclusive partner still triggers the *partner's* jealousy. Get close and you find the serenity is trained: Frankie grew up heavy — a childhood they describe only as "gravity ran in the family" — and the low-G thing started as escape.
- **Is-this-boring test:** Frankie hasn't let their own body experience full gravity in four years. The arc where they finally stand at normal weight — and shake — is the quietest gut-punch in the game.

### 3. August Vang — "REVERB" *(SLICE)*
- **Age:** 25.
- **Power kit — Sonic (sound sculpting):** August hears everything and can throw it back. **Core moves:** *Feedback Lash* (a cutting directional shriek — the poke tool), *Dead Air* (a silence dome: enemies inside can't call reinforcements, casters can't cast, and the fight goes eerily mute — a design showpiece), *Bassline* (a rhythmic ground-pulse that staggers enemies on the beat and buffs squad attack speed if THEY attack on the beat). **Signature/finisher — "Encore":** records the last 5 seconds of combat audio and replays it as concussive force — the bigger the fight was, the bigger the blast.
- **Control contract:** Equipped default: *Feedback Lash*; *Dead Air*/*Bassline* unlock at Signature-Evolution tree nodes; loadout swappable at base.
- **Personality:** watchful in a way people mistake for shy; devastating one-liners delivered at library volume; holds grudges in perfect fidelity because he literally cannot forget a sound.
- **Day job / hangout:** live sound engineer at **The Foundry**, the black-box music venue on Hennepin. Between sets he's at the soundboard; after close he's on the loading dock listening to the city like it's a mix he's not happy with.
- **Recruitment:** friendship, not spectacle. August has watched capes grandstand his whole life and is unimpressed. He joins after the player does three small things quietly — no witnesses, no rep gain — and he heard every one of them.
- **Romance/friendship hook:** *Open.* August is the slow build: months of dry jokes across the soundboard, then one night he plays you a track he made of city sounds and it's — obviously, embarrassingly — about you. His baggage: he's the eldest son and his parents think the power is a phase between him and a pharmacy degree. He's never told them he can hear their arguments from three blocks away.
- **Is-this-boring test:** he knows everyone's heartbeat like a signature. In one scene he identifies a disguised villain in a crowd of forty because "your pulse lies worse than your face." He knows when the player's heart rate spikes around him. He's too polite to mention it. For now.

### 4. Ingrid Halvorsen — "WHITEOUT" *(SLICE — protected: the Ingrid–Petra geometry ships intact.)*
- **Age:** 34.
- **Power kit — Cold (the most Minnesotan power in the game):** not "ice blasts" — *heat theft.* **Core moves:** *Draw* (siphons heat from an enemy: they slow, she gains armor frost), *Black Ice* (paints a ground patch — enemies slide, her squad skates: allies crossing it get a free dash), *Shelter* (a snow-squall dome that blocks projectiles both ways — the "okay everybody breathe" button). **Signature/finisher — "January":** drops the local temperature to lethal-cold for six seconds; everything hostile freezes mid-pose like a photograph of a blizzard. **Binding differentiation:** January deals **no damage**, and each freeze **breaks on hit** — control, not clear; the squad chooses which statues matter. Conversations still end.
- **Control contract:** Equipped default: *Draw*; *Black Ice*/*Shelter* unlock at Signature-Evolution tree nodes; loadout swappable at base.
- **Personality:** economical with words, extravagant with actions; competitive only where nobody can see; the person everyone assumes is fine, and never is.
- **Day job / hangout:** manages the **Uptown ice arena**; drives the Zamboni herself at 11pm because it's the only smooth thing in her life. Former pro hockey — one season, one injury, one long silence about it.
- **Recruitment:** the arena's rink chiller dies mid-heatwave and she's been keeping the ice frozen *herself*, secretly, eighteen hours a day, and it's killing her. Notice. Fix the chiller or get the money for it. She joins because you saw her working — she'll never say that's why.
- **Romance/friendship hook:** *Oriented (women), Exclusive.* Ingrid loves like she plays defense — total commitment, zero announcement. Her ex-wife Petra teaches yoga four blocks away (see Independents) and the divorce paperwork is done but the eye contact isn't. Get close and you learn the injury that ended hockey wasn't an accident: she Drew heat mid-game, on camera, in the era before that was survivable.
- **Is-this-boring test:** she is physically warm to the touch — always — because the cold isn't hers, it's everyone else's, held. The first time she lets a partner feel her actually cold hands is the most intimate fade-to-black in the game.

### 5. Theo Park — "STITCH" *(SLICE)*
- **Age:** 24.
- **Power kit — Speedster (short-burst blinkwork):** not a marathon runner — a sewing machine. **Core moves:** *Needle* (a straight-line dash-through with a slash on exit), *Basting* (tags up to four enemies at walking pace, then hits all tags at once), *Errand* (instantly repositions one ALLY instead of himself — the only speedster whose speed is a gift). **Signature/finisher — "Seam Ripper":** ten dashes stitched through the whole enemy formation in one second; the screen holds a comic-panel freeze of every pass before the damage lands.
- **Control contract:** Equipped default: *Needle*; *Basting*/*Errand* unlock at Signature-Evolution tree nodes; loadout swappable at base.
- **Personality:** chronically early and emotionally late; funny as a defense mechanism, then accidentally sincere; treats every kindness like a debt he has to repay at 200%.
- **Day job / hangout:** delivery rider for half the restaurants in Uptown — everyone knows Theo, Theo knows everyone's order. Refuels at **Big Ronda's Nite Owl** diner counter at 2am, vibrating slightly.
- **Recruitment:** cheap to recruit, hard to keep. He says yes immediately — too immediately — and the actual recruitment is the follow-up mission where you learn he's said yes to two factions already and is quadruple-booked out of pure inability to disappoint anyone. Make him quit something. That's the price.
- **Romance/friendship hook:** *Open.* Theo flirts at full speed and retreats faster; the first real date, he shows up an hour early and does two laps of the lake to burn the nerves off, and you know because a jogger gossips about it. Baggage: his parents run a dry cleaner in St. Paul and think he's in grad school. The lie is eleven months old and compounding.
- **Is-this-boring test:** his perception of a romantic moment lasts, subjectively, about an hour. He has experienced every kiss of his life in slow motion, alone, and has never once told anyone that. **Staged (binding):** his Romance-3 almost-moment plays at **0.1x time — the game's only non-combat slow-motion scene** — and when time snaps back, he tells you. Breaking the "never once told anyone" IS the scene.

### 6. Maggie St. Pierre — "BALLAST" *(SLICE — protected: do not soften her.)*
- **Age:** 44. Granddaughter of Alfred St. Pierre — Ely Deep, 1953 (`09` §3); the first generation treats her as one of their own two generations early, and the Range Rules she carries are inherited, not adopted.
- **Power kit — Density tank:** Maggie doesn't dodge. **Core moves:** *Set* (roots in place, density up: damage taken drops 80%, enemies who melee her stagger themselves), *Beam Walk* (a slow unstoppable advance that shoves the whole enemy front line back — territory control as a punch), *Spotter's Call* (taunts everything in a cone; the squad takes zero damage from taunted enemies for 4 seconds). **Signature/finisher — "Topping Out":** maximum density, one jump, one landing. Unconditional knockdown on everything — **binding differentiation: the longest windup in the game**; the squad's job is buying her the seconds, and everyone in Uptown knows the pose by now. Car alarms three blocks over, filed by the gossip system under "Ballast again."
- **Control contract:** Equipped default: *Set*; *Beam Walk*/*Spotter's Call* unlock at Signature-Evolution tree nodes; loadout swappable at base.
- **Personality:** unshockable; generous with time, stingy with sympathy ("you'll live"); the driest one-liners in the roster, delivered at load-bearing volume.
- **Day job / hangout:** union ironworker, Local 512 — currently on the Hennepin corridor build. After shift: **Moe's** bar, back booth, one beer, the crossword, in pen.
- **Recruitment:** work. She won't join a squad she hasn't stood a shift with. Take three faction jobs alongside her as a non-recruit ally first; if you cut corners on any of them, the third job never comes.
- **Romance/friendship hook:** **Friendship-only, and the richest arc for it.** Maggie's wife died four winters ago — the first-snow event lands on their anniversary, and Maggie's scene that night is the reason the friendship track exists. She is Ojibwe, White Earth descent, raised half in the city and half up north; she will feed you, insult you, teach you to fix drywall, and tell you exactly which of your romantic decisions is stupid, in order. Everyone in the roster is a little in love with her. She knows. It's not like that.
- **Is-this-boring test:** she walked out of the building collapse that killed two coworkers in '19 by holding a floor up for six minutes, and has attended both families' Christmases every year since — one of those families is Moe's (see Independents). She has never once called herself a hero and gets quietly, dangerously angry when the press does.

### 7. Yusuf Ali — "MERIDIAN" *(POST-SLICE: his recruitment gates behind Commons rep tier 3 — deeper faction sim than the slice ships.)*
- **Age:** 30.
- **Power kit — Telekinetic (precision, not poltergeist):** Yusuf is a physical therapist; his TK works like his hands — exact. **Core moves:** *Pin* (locks one enemy's limb mid-swing; timing turns hits into whiffs), *Traction* (drags an enemy out of formation to the squad — the pick tool), *Alignment* (a squad buff: everyone's next attack auto-crits because he *nudged* it two degrees true). **Signature/finisher — "Manipulation Under Anesthesia":** briefly puppets the biggest enemy on the field as an ally. He apologizes to them afterward. Every time.
- **Control contract:** Equipped default: *Pin*; *Traction*/*Alignment* unlock at Signature-Evolution tree nodes; loadout swappable at base.
- **Personality:** courteous in a way that makes rude people feel it; principled and unbothered about the cost; a chess hustler's patience — he's always three moves in, even in small talk.
- **Day job / hangout:** physical therapist at a Lyn-Lake clinic; half his clients are uninsured and billed in ambiguity. Found at **Farhia's Halal Market** (his aunt's — see Independents) restocking shelves he doesn't work at, or playing speed chess at the lake tables, taking money off tourists politely.
- **Recruitment:** through the community, not to him. Yusuf joins when the Commons faction (see Bee Toliver) vouches for you at rep tier 3 — he trusts Bee's read on people over his own, which is itself a story he'll only tell you at friendship max.
- **Romance/friendship hook:** *Open, Exclusive, demisexual slow burn* — Yusuf doesn't do sparks, he does foundations; his romance track has the fewest flirt options and the most weight per line. Baggage: an almost-engagement two years ago that his family still mourns louder than he does, and a brother in Nairobi he sends money to and lies to about how.
- **Is-this-boring test:** he can feel the exact weight of anything he looks at — and once held a collapsing pedestrian bridge for nine minutes with 62 people on it, then went home before the news arrived. August is the only person who knows, because August heard his heartbeat that day: dead calm.

### 8. Camille Dubois — "KILN" *(SLICE)*
- **Age:** 33.
- **Power kit — Fire/heat (craft, not carnage):** Camille shapes heat like glass. **Core moves:** *Glasswork* (pulls molten barriers out of the air — cover on demand that enemies burn on), *Anneal* (a heat-wrap on an ally that converts 50% of damage they take into a delayed heal — the game's weirdest, best support tool, and by design the ONLY heal in the game: a conversion, never a pool), *Flashpoint* (detonates any of her cooling glass structures). **Signature/finisher — "Studio Fire":** an expanding ring of shimmering heat-haze; enemies inside take ramping burn and — the flourish — their weapons visibly droop and slag.
- **Control contract:** Equipped default: *Glasswork*; *Anneal*/*Flashpoint* unlock at Signature-Evolution tree nodes; loadout swappable at base.
- **Personality:** loud, precise, and warm, in that order; flirts as punctuation; New Orleans to the bone and openly at war with Minnesota winter ("y'all *chose* this?").
- **Day job / hangout:** glassblower with a studio garage off 26th, sells at craft fairs, teaches a Thursday class that has a waitlist because of the arms, and she knows it. Post-studio: **The Velvet Antler**, where Cass Delane comps her drinks and she lets him think that means something.
- **Recruitment:** her studio's lease is a pawn in the Aldermen–Isles Trust land fight (see Factions). Secure the building — buy it, leverage it, or burn the right document (her suggestion, delivered while making direct eye contact and a very thin wine glass).
- **Romance/friendship hook:** *Open, non-exclusive — but unlike Frankie's, Camille's non-exclusivity has a live wire in it: PRIMACY.* She makes no exclusivity demand and gives jealous players a kind, unmovable speech on date one — but she tracks who she is to you, and being demoted from *"the one she calls first"* triggers her unique consequence scene: no shouting, one glass piece delivered to your base, unsigned, flawless, and cold. Get close and the volume drops: she left New Orleans after the flood took the family studio, and every piece she blows is one her grandmother taught her, from memory, in order. She's on piece 214 of an unknown total.
- **Is-this-boring test:** she cannot be burned but can be cold, and Minnesota winter is the first thing in her life that's ever been genuinely dangerous to her. Her first-snow-event scene — furious, laughing, wrapped in four coats, making the snow steam around her — is the roster's best comedy beat with teeth.

### 9. Dominic Reyes — "SLIP" *(POST-SLICE: his recruitment leans on Isles Trust blackmail content scheduled with the Trust's endgame arc.)*
- **Age:** 28.
- **Power kit — Phasing (the un-wall):** Dom stops being solid; matter files a complaint. **Core moves:** *Ghost Step* (phase through an attack with a counter-window on re-solidify), *Doorman* (phases a SECTION OF WALL for 3 seconds — the whole squad flanks through architecture), *Pickpocket* (phases a hand into an enemy: steals the item, the weapon, or — on brutes — a satisfying *click* that disables armor). **Signature/finisher — "Half In":** re-solidifies with an arm inside a wall, floor, or vehicle and *anchors* the fight's biggest problem to it. Comic-splash-page rule: this move always frames well and he always checks his hair after.
- **Control contract:** Equipped default: *Ghost Step*; *Doorman*/*Pickpocket* unlock at Signature-Evolution tree nodes; loadout swappable at base.
- **Personality:** charm as a locksmith's tool — every conversation, he's feeling for pins; vain, and funny about it; ethical in a code entirely of his own drafting.
- **Day job / hangout:** locksmith and escape-room designer ("both directions of the same skill"). Shop on Hennepin; takes his coffee across the street at the record store. **Staging note:** his idle animation there is flirting with the clientele *via their reflections in the shop window* — loopable, no dialogue cost, reads at a glance.
- **Recruitment:** he's being blackmailed. The Isles Trust has proof of his old career — three years of extremely artistic burglary, Isles mansions exclusively — and they want him back on the payroll. Get the leverage off him. How you do it (steal it back with him, buy it, trade it) sets his whole arc's temperature.
- **Romance/friendship hook:** *Oriented (men), jealous in denial* — Dom performs cool detachment and then phases through a wall "coincidentally" whenever your dates walk past his shop; the gossip system rats him out and it's delicious. Baggage: the burglary years had a partner, and the partner didn't retire, and every so often a mansion job in the papers has Dom's old signature move in it, done sloppier.
- **Is-this-boring test:** he has never in his life been locked in, out, or up — and is more claustrophobic than any character in the game. Not of spaces. Of situations. The romance arc is watching a man who can walk through walls learn to stay in a room.

### 10. Priya Chandrasekhar — "BROWNOUT" *(POST-SLICE: Rolling Blackout's block-wide light state is a renderer feature slated after the slice.)*
- **Age:** 35.
- **Power kit — Electricity (the grid made flesh):** utility-scale, utility-tempered. **Core moves:** *Arc Bite* (a chaining jolt that jumps to enemies who are touching or adjacent — punishes formations), *Load Shed* (drains charge from anything electrical nearby into a shield — in Uptown, that's every fight), *Live Wire* (drops a crackling line on the ground; enemies crossing it lock up mid-step). **Signature/finisher — "Rolling Blackout":** kills every light on the block for five seconds — enemies blind, her squad sees by her glow, and the neighborhood files it under "Priya's having a week."
- **Control contract:** Equipped default: *Arc Bite*; *Load Shed*/*Live Wire* unlock at Signature-Evolution tree nodes; loadout swappable at base.
- **Personality:** blunt as a fuse box; competence-arrogant and earned; secretly sentimental and would rather be electrocuted than caught at it.
- **Day job / hangout:** journeyman electrician, half her jobs off-book for neighbors who can't afford code. Drinks one bourbon at Moe's on Fridays, fixes his neon sign every time, refuses payment, insults the wiring.
- **Recruitment:** money and respect, in that order. She's the only hero with a straightforward hiring negotiation — and lowballing her locks the recruit for a full season. She has a rate. The rate is the personality test.
- **Romance/friendship hook:** *Open, Exclusive — the most jealous partner in the game, and the most honest about it:* "I notice everything with a current in it, and you, sweetheart, run about ninety millivolts." Divorced; her ex-husband got the house, the dog, and the version of her that apologized. Get close and you find the sentimental core: she keeps every broken thing she's ever fixed for someone who didn't come back — a full shelf of them, in her unit in **the Fourplex (03 #15)**.
- **Is-this-boring test:** she can feel the whole neighborhood's power draw like a pulse — she knows who's home, who left the stove on, whose grow-op is whose — and treats that omniscience with a lineworker's total professional discretion. The one time she breaks that code is a story arc, and it's because someone's pattern looked like her own worst year.

## 2. Faction Leaders (4 factions — 3 hold corners, 1 holds the paper)

Uptown's turf is a **three-way cold war** — the Iron Range Crew, the Commons, and the Aldermen each hold a corner of the map (turf boundaries live in `03-world-minneapolis.md`) — **and the Isles Trust holds the mortgage on all three corners.** Every faction offers real value; every faction costs you standing with at least one other. Rep is earned in public — the gossip system is the rep system's nervous system. All four factions have skin in the Rogue Zone arc (the Aldermen signed the CIVIS contract, the Trust holds its paper, the Commons runs the corridor's warning grid, the Crew smuggles through the perimeter at cost) — entanglements and endgames in `09-story-lore.md` §6.

### CASS DELANE — The Iron Range Crew (nightlife, muscle, freight)
- **Profile:** 44. The Crew runs Uptown's after-dark economy — clubs, freight that skips paperwork, muscle with Iron Range surnames who train at **Skål Gym**. Cass's front office is **The Velvet Antler** (venue #05 in 03) — taxidermy-chic club, magenta light, a VIP mezzanine everyone claims to hate and fights to get into. Cass is seduction as infrastructure: he remembers your drink, your ex, and your price, and quotes all three back at the exact wrong moment. **Personality:** patient, theatrical, transactional to the marrow — but pays debts to the penny, both kinds. **The dangerous part:** the Antler's back room runs a card game and a smuggling cache behind the cooler, and half of Uptown's off-book goods move through Crew garages. (The supers-only speakeasy, The Understory — #24 in 03 — is NOT his; it's neutral ground, and its neutrality is the one line even Cass won't test.) He has never thrown a punch; people volunteer.
- **Turf:** the Greenway trench, the 28th St spine, and the garage block. Note the geography joke the whole neighborhood is in on: his front office sits on the Hennepin spine — *inside Aldermen turf.* He pays rent, in every sense.
- **Offers the player:** club jobs (bouncing, debt collection with style clauses, "escort this idiot home alive"), the best fence/vendor in the game, after-dark intel on every other faction, and **followers:** Crew staff for the base — door staff, drivers, lookouts. Staffing and mission-support flavor, per 02: followers are not combat units; they make your businesses run and your night jobs smoother.
- **Romance (COMMITTED):** Cass has a **full romance track — the hardest gates in the game.** He'll flirt like a five-alarm fire for free; the track itself opens only behind top-tier Crew rep, one kept secret, and one refused bribe. The arc is discovering whether there's a person under the proprietor. There is. He hates it.
- **Rep rises:** completing night jobs, spending big, keeping his secrets, being interesting. **Rep falls:** violence inside the club (bad for business), talking to cops or the Aldermen, being boring — genuinely, repeated dull dialogue choices tick rep down. Cass's respect is a bandwidth issue.
- **Conflicts:** Odegaard is his landlord in the only sense that matters — the Antler sits on the Aldermen's spine, and the inspection shakedowns are turf pressure with a clipboard; Bee considers him a landlord of vices; the Isles Trust launders through the Crew and he's one audit from owned.

### BERNICE "BEE" TOLIVER — The Commons (the neighbors)
- **Profile:** 61. Runs the **Lyndale Commons co-op and garden network** — and, informally, the phone tree that can put forty neighbors on a corner in eleven minutes, several of them retired supers who "don't do that anymore" until they do. Bee marched here in the 60s as a child on her mother's hip and has outlasted every developer since. **Personality:** warm like a kitchen, ruthless like a chess clock; keeps receipts going back decades; her hugs and her ultimatums use the same arms. **The militia:** the Casserole Brigade is a joke name the neighbors use in public and absolutely not a joke.
- **Turf:** the lakefront — parkway, beach, pier, and the Pavilion — plus the garden block. HQ: **The Shorehouse** (#18 in 03). **The phone tree is a named gossip-system exception:** Commons-relevant topics broadcast to ALL Commons NPCs in one sleep tick, ignoring normal hop propagation. Nothing else in the game moves that fast. Bee built it decades before anyone here had powers.
- **Offers the player:** the farming system's best seeds and crop contracts, cheap base food/healing vendors, recruitment access (Yusuf gates behind her), and **followers:** neighbor-watch volunteers — base staffing and mission-support flavor: watchers, feeders, fixers, endless morale, free upkeep because they're fed by the garden your crops feed.
- **Rep rises:** garden work, defending shops, showing up TWICE (Bee's rule: anyone can show up once), giving generously in public. **Rep falls:** property damage in residential blocks, working for the Aldermen, romancing-and-ghosting a neighbor (the phone tree hits every Commons ear before you're home).
- **Conflicts:** existential war with Odegaard (rezoning) and the Isles Trust (buying duplexes for cash); complicated truce with Cass — he hires her college kids for summer jobs, day shifts, front of house, and she pretends not to know what's behind the cooler.

### COUNCILMEMBER GRANT ODEGAARD — The Aldermen (the machine)
- **Profile:** 52. Fourth-term councilmember, smile like a ribbon-cutting, handshake like a subpoena. "The Ward Office" is the colloquial name of his HQ operation at **The Caucus Room** (#21 in 03) — nominally a supper club, functionally a permit mill. Odegaard is corruption in its most Minnesotan form: nothing so vulgar as a bribe — just permits that stall, inspections that materialize, and a nonprofit run by his brother-in-law. **Personality:** passive-aggression as a martial art; genuinely believes he IS the neighborhood; never angry, only "disappointed, frankly." **His capes:** **Civic Shield LLC**, a private super-security firm on city retainer — matching windbreakers, body cameras that malfunction on schedule.
- **Turf:** the Hennepin commercial spine — which means every storefront on it, including, deliciously, Cass's front office.
- **Offers the player:** the highest-paying jobs in the game (with the dirtiest fine print), permit fast-tracking for base upgrades that other factions gate, legal cover after messy fights, and **followers:** Civic Shield contractors — the most professional base staffing and mission-support hires available, and they unionize against YOU at low loyalty, which is the joke and the design.
- **Rep rises:** doing his cleanup work, photo ops, delivering dirt on other factions. **Rep falls:** public embarrassments, siding with Bee at council meetings, any recorded contradiction of his version of events. Uniquely: his rep NEVER fully decays — Odegaard forgives nothing and forgets nothing, he just re-prices you.
- **Conflicts:** squeezing Cass through inspections of the Antler — landlord-turf pressure dressed as public safety; rezoning Bee out of existence; publicly at war with the Isles Trust and privately their errand boy, which is the campaign's best-kept open secret.

### ADELAIDE WRAY — The Isles Trust (old money, cold water)
- **Profile:** 70. Matriarch of a flour-fortune family whose name is on two museums and zero indictments. Runs the **Isles Trust** from a Lake of the Isles mansion with staff, hedges, and a dock nobody swims from. **Personality:** courteous like a blade in a velvet box; finds open ambition vulgar and open emotion worse; collects people the way the family collected art — provenance mattering more than beauty. **The syndicate:** the Trust owns paper — mortgages, liens, blackmail (see: Dominic), city debt — and hires talent only through three layers of lawyers. Adelaide's one visible vice: she attends every funeral in Uptown, invited or not, and no one has ever had the nerve to ask why. (One person knows the older half of the answer. See Dre.)
- **Territory (binding):** **The Trust is non-territorial: it holds paper, not corners — no banners, no patrols. Its rep track gates capital, auctions, and society access.** The other three factions fight over blocks; the Trust owns the concept of blocks.
- **Offers the player:** capital — the only faction that funds base expansion outright; rare-goods auctions; society access (the mansion parties are a whole flirtation economy in tuxedos); and **followers:** discreet "estate security" — elite base staff, few in number, and they report on you to her, always, forever.
- **Relationship track (not romance):** Adelaide has opinion tiers like anyone — and hers are **nearly immovable.** Money doesn't move them; discretion moves them by inches. Reaching even WARM with Adelaide is **endgame-tier social content**, and what she gives you there isn't affection — it's provenance: she starts telling you true things about everyone, including, eventually, the funerals.
- **Rep rises:** discretion, profitable partnerships, favors done without being told twice, defeating people she dislikes *quietly*. **Rep falls:** publicity of any kind involving her name, sentimentality in negotiations, and — steepest penalty in the game — telling anyone what she's asked of you.
- **Conflicts:** buying the ground out from under Bee block by block; holds Odegaard's leash and lets him bark at her in public for appearances; regards Cass as "the help who forgot" — the Trust holds the Antler building's paper, and with it the mineral rights to his soul.

## 4. The NPC Archetype System (making 100 strangers feel like people)

The ~80–120 ambient NPCs are built from **archetype templates × personality dials × two quirks.** The goal: any NPC the player squints at should survive the squint.

**Tone hardening (binding):** All ambient NPCs generate as unambiguous adults — **age floor 21 in model, voice, and writing.** Flirt-flagging is additionally denied to any archetype roll below stated thresholds (BOLDNESS < 40 or WARMTH < 30 never generates flirt lines; flirtation stays opt-in and characterful per the tone bible).

### Dials (0–100, rolled per NPC within archetype ranges)
- **WARMTH** — default goodwill. Governs greeting tier, forgiveness rate, gift reactions.
- **BOLDNESS** — will they approach the player, flirt first, run toward or from a fight.
- **CHATTINESS** — line length, gossip volume, interrupt frequency.
- **MISCHIEF** — lie rate, tease rate, whether their gossip mutates (see §6).

### Archetype templates (name / dial ranges / voice note)
| Archetype | Warmth | Boldness | Chat | Mischief | Voice note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Young Professional | 40–70 | 30–60 | 40–70 | 20–40 | Optimizes everything, including this conversation. |
| Punk | 30–60 | 60–90 | 30–70 | 60–90 | Aggressively kind under aggressive everything else. |
| Retiree | 50–90 | 40–80 | 60–95 | 30–70 | Has time, uses it on you; passive-aggression fluent. |
| Jogger | 40–70 | 50–80 | 20–50 | 10–30 | Talks in intervals; gossip delivered mid-stride. |
| Barfly | 30–70 | 40–70 | 70–95 | 50–80 | Moe's Greek chorus; memory excellent before 10pm. |
| Student (21+) | 40–75 | 20–70 | 50–85 | 40–75 | Everything is either "so real" or a crisis; no middle. |
| Co-op Parent | 45–80 | 30–60 | 55–90 | 20–50 | Weaponized "just wondering"; Bee's info militia. |
| Service Lifer | 35–70 | 40–75 | 30–65 | 40–70 | Seen everything twice; tips define their whole read of you. |
| Lake Rat | 50–85 | 55–85 | 40–75 | 30–60 | Shirtless in April; the shoreline's ambient witnesses. |
| Church/Mosque Auntie | 55–90 | 50–85 | 65–95 | 25–60 | Blesses you and audits you in one sentence. |
| Softball Leaguer | 45–75 | 50–80 | 50–80 | 40–70 | All social capital denominated in beer league standings. |
| Gutter Poet | 30–65 | 60–95 | 20–90 | 55–95 | Busker/oracle; occasionally, accidentally, correct. |

Each ambient NPC also rolls **two quirks** from a pool (~60 entries: "always eating something," "quotes their therapist," "hates one specific hero on principle," "calls everyone 'chief,'" "visibly rooting for your worst romance option"...). Dials + quirks + archetype voice = no two strangers read identical, at template cost.

### Promotion: from ambient to named ("the sim remembers them")
- **Track:** every ambient NPC keeps a lightweight interaction count and a flag for high-impact events (you saved them, fought beside/against them, flirted, insulted, gifted, stood near one of your fade-to-blacks).
- **Trigger:** 3 meaningful interactions OR 1 high-impact event → **promotion.** The NPC receives: a generated name (culturally coherent with their model), a home anchor, a schedule (two anchors + commute), one relationship link to an existing named NPC ("Dre's cousin," "rides Marcus's bus"), and **one secret** rolled from a table — the secret is what makes them worth a second conversation.
- **Recognition dialogue flips:** post-promotion they reference the promoting event forever ("You're the one who caught the awning. I tell people that.").
- **Caps:** max ~15 player-promoted NPCs live at once; the least-interacted demote gracefully (they move, cite a reason, gossip about it once).
- **The payoff rule:** any promoted NPC can later surface in content — as a gossip source, a date's plus-one, a witness at a faction hearing, or **the only possible witness to Moe's Tuesdays (§3)** — the flagship proof that promotion is the sim honoring pillar 1: *nothing is set dressing.*

## 5. Memory & Opinion — The Voice Bible

Opinion is a scale: **GRUDGE → WARY → NEUTRAL → WARM → CLOSE → SMITTEN.** Every line the dialogue system assembles carries (a) opinion tier, (b) up to two memory tags, (c) the speaker's dials. Rules for writers: memory references are *specific, never summarized* ("you caught the awning," never "you helped me"); higher opinion buys longer sentences and worse jokes; GRUDGE tier is short, cold, and never cartoonish; flirtation only generates at WARM+ AND player-initiated history (tone bible: opt-in, always).

**Tier formula (binding here; math owned by the sim doc):** `tier = band(Trust + Respect)` — two axes, summed, cut into the six bands. **SMITTEN additionally requires Attraction ≥ threshold AND an active romance track** — no track, no SMITTEN, whatever the numbers say. Opinion tiers **derive from** 02's friendship/romance levels — one scale derives from the other; they are never authored separately and never allowed to drift.

## 6. Gossip Rules (the city's nervous system)

**What spreads, ranked by juice (spread speed multiplier):**
1. **Romance & betrayal (×3):** who left with whom, who got caught, who cried at the Nite Owl. Fade-to-blacks are private; *arrivals and departures are not.*
2. **Fights (×2.5):** wins travel fast; LOSSES travel faster with better details. Property damage adds Bee's phone tree to the route.
3. **Betrayals of factions (×2.5):** flipping on a faction is permanent-record gossip; it never fully decays, it re-prices (Odegaard rule, city-wide).
4. **Generosity (×1.5, slow but sticky):** kindness spreads slower, decays slowest, and compounds — three separate generosity rumors merge into a reputation trait ("good tipper energy").
5. **Weirdness (×1):** schedule anomalies, Isles trips at 3am, whatever Ballast's car-alarm shockwave was this time.

**Mechanics:**
- **Hop propagation:** gossip hops person-to-person along real schedule overlaps — no broadcast. **One named exception:** Bee's phone tree (§2) broadcasts Commons-relevant topics to all Commons NPCs in one sleep tick.
- **Hubs** (Dre ×3, Moe ×2, Marcus's bus ×2, the barbershop chairs, the lake path) multiply spread.
- **Fidelity, corroborators, and mutators:** every item carries a fidelity score. **Low-MISCHIEF witnesses are corroborators** — passing through them RAISES fidelity and can attach a true detail ("one-handed," confirmed). **High-MISCHIEF carriers are mutators** — each hop through one drops fidelity a notch and warps a detail (a lost fight grows a torn shirt).
- **Mutation FORKS:** a mutated item doesn't replace the original — **both versions propagate**, fidelity marking the fork. The player can encounter, and separately have to live down, two versions of the same night.
- **Sinks (generalized rule):** any NPC can be a per-topic sink. Wren sinks *everything* (the absolute case). **Faction-affiliated NPCs sink gossip damaging to their own faction** — Crew staff don't pass Antler back-room items; Commons neighbors don't spread Bee's business; Civic Shield contractors' recollections malfunction on schedule, like their cameras.
- **World triggers:** gossip reaching a **flagged NPC** can fire a world event, not just a dialogue change. Canonical example: any item about the player crossing Trust interests that reaches an estate-security ear fires Adelaide's response beat (see chain 5).
- **Inferred provenance ("the behavioral tell"):** high-observation NPCs (Ronda, Moe, Farhia, Marcus) can emit gossip items generated from *visible state changes* rather than witnessed events — a changed seating chart, a standing order poured unprompted, credit quietly cut off. Inferred items carry `provenance: inferred`, a hard fidelity cap, and never name the trigger event — the room knows *something*, not *what*.
- **Speed:** a typical juicy item saturates Uptown in ~2 in-game days; it hits your partner in hours if any hop touches a hub.

**Five example chains:**
1. **The Awning:** You catch a falling awning over two brunchers → Jogger (MISCHIEF 15, corroborator: fidelity UP) → lake path → Kai (adds "one-handed," true — corroborated detail) → Dre's Saturday chair → by Monday, Cass comps your first drink "for the show." *Generosity + weirdness, ×1.5, fully saturated in 3 days, unlocks a promotion candidate: one of the brunchers.*
2. **The Good Booth** *(systemic — inferred provenance):* You stand up your CLOSE-tier date at the Nite Owl → Ronda tells no one, but *changes the seating chart* — you two are never sat together again → the chart change emits an inferred item (fidelity-capped, trigger unnamed) → the Barflies read the room like a headline → Moe corroborates nothing but pours accordingly → your date's friends go WARY across the board in 36 hours. *No one gossiped, exactly. The room did — and the system can say so without knowing why.*
3. **The Windbreaker Job:** You quietly do a cleanup job for the Aldermen → a Civic Shield contractor's bodycam "malfunctions" but the Co-op Parent across the street doesn't → Bee's phone tree broadcast by dinner (the named exception, at full speed) → Commons rep −2 tiers before you've even been paid → Bee's next greeting is calibration line #1's colder cousin.
4. **The Two-Timers' Waltz:** You're exclusive with Marisol and get seen leaving the Antler at 3am with a dancer's jacket over your shoulders → Barfly (MISCHIEF 70, mutator) → the item FORKS: the true version (jacket) and the mutation (her jacket AND his helmet) both propagate, fidelity diverging → Moe hears both, passes neither, pours Marisol's usual *without being asked* — which she clocks instantly (inferred provenance, weaponized) → Marisol confronts you mid-derby-practice, publicly, on skates. You're now living down two rumors, one of them false, and the false one is stickier.
5. **The Funeral Guest:** You spot Adelaide Wray at a stranger's funeral and tell one person → the chain runs TOWARD the Isles for once → dies at every Trust-adjacent hop (per-topic sinks, doing their job) → Wren, of all people, mentions she's heard it — meaning it crossed the whole city underground → the item reaches a **flagged** estate-security ear → world trigger: an estate-security follower appears in your base's staffing rotation *unrequested.* Some gossip is a message you sent without knowing. Adelaide answers all her mail.

**Act-reveal & zone chains** (the main arc IS the gossip system's stress test — `09` §5; one new rule canonized there applies system-wide: **mutations can be accidentally true.** A high-MISCHIEF hop can invent, as embellishment, something that happens to BE a later stage's truth — it propagates at fidelity-zero, and neither the system nor the city knows the difference. This is intended horror, not a bug.):

6. **The Handshake Photo (Stage 1):** Brynn Kowalczyk leaks the Eagan visitor log → the Gopher Line runs it nightly (mutation factory: Dale's calls fork it three ways in a week, and one fork — "he BUILT their list" — is accidentally true at fidelity zero) → Dre refuses to amplify (named-hub curation: the chair is Switzerland, and the item arrives at his chair and *stops*) → Odegaard's presser picks the least-true fork on purpose → world trigger: `heard_the_handshake_photo` reaching any Civic Shield ear adds a windbreaker tail to the player's schedule for a week.
7. **The Recitation (zone):** a damaged unit recites its flag queue at the perimeter → a Lake Rat livestreams it (low MISCHIEF, corroborator: fidelity UP, names accurate — which is exactly the problem) → the item splits by audience: corridor NPCs gain `family_flagged_by_civis` context (grief-class handling, no juice multiplier — the system does not let the neighborhood's fear be entertainment, per the controversy contract) while the rest of the city runs it as ×2.5 weirdness → Marcus reroutes the 21 around the perimeter unprompted (inferred provenance: the whole city reads the detour).
8. **The Registry Broadcast (Stage 3, maximum load):** Naz cracks the targeting format → hand-carries it to Bee (one hop, no mutation — he's a corroborator by temperament and knows it) → **Commons phone-tree broadcast: every Commons NPC re-tiers in one sleep tick**, the named exception at full load → `knows_the_registry_truth` plants city-wide within two days, never decays, re-prices everything → world triggers cascade: Imelda's authored scene arms, Adelaide sends the player a single card reading "Now we can talk," and Bee's confrontation (out-of-system) fires the next time the player crosses the garden block. The morning after is the quietest walk to Uptown in the game, and every greeting on it is this doc, running.
